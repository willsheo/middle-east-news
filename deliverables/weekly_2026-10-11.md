# Middle East Weekly Review

**October 5 to October 11, 2026**

- **Week in brief:** The Houthi and Saudi war overtook Hormuz as the week's driver, with repeated Houthi strikes on Riyadh's King Khalid airport, airline suspensions, a Yemeni ground offensive on the Red Sea coast and, by Saturday, Trump saying the US "may" join Saudi strikes on the Houthis, while his pledge of no strikes on Iran before the November 3 midterms held and Iran said it had revised its proposals; Brent ended the week near $104.7, up about 2%, and KOSPI fell about 5% in three sessions despite Samsung's record quarter.

---

## 1. The Week's Arc

The week began with Brent at $100.32 on October 5 after Aramco's Asia price cut and a recovery in Gulf exports, and with the Yemeni government launching a ground and naval offensive on the Red Sea coast (confirmed October 6). Houthi claims on Riyadh and Rabigh followed, and Saudi authorities confirmed attacks on Jizan and Najran airports. The contest over Mokha and the Bab el-Mandeb coast stayed disputed all week, each side claiming control.

Hormuz tanker attacks set records on October 8 (UKMTO counted nine in the first six days of October), and Brent spiked about 5% intraday toward $105 alongside Gulf shut-ins from Hurricane Isaias. Trump then posted that the US will not attack Iran before November 3 and Pezeshkian said Iran had revised its proposals, while the NYT reported a drafted three day US strike campaign that Trump has not approved. The structural change is the pairing of a restraint pledge with open planning for the period after the election.

The war's centre of gravity moved to Riyadh. Houthi attacks on King Khalid airport on October 8 and again on October 10 killed civilians (counts conflict), closed the airport, and led Lufthansa, Emirates, Etihad and others to suspend flights, with 324 cancellations on October 10. On Saturday Trump said the US may join the Saudi campaign against the Houthis. Russia's promise of 300,000 tonnes of diesel, a Treasury waiver and a German 15 million barrel release answered the price level, not the war, and the Kremlin said Putin relayed Iran's views to Trump.
