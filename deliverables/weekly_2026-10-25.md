# Middle East Weekly Review

**October 19 to October 25, 2026**

- **Week in brief:** The Houthi war on Saudi Arabia reached Riyadh's defense ministry, Dammam airport, Yanbu and Abha and forced Aramco to evacuate its Dhahran headquarters, Brent settled at $116.72, up about 6.2% on the week and the highest settle since April 29, KOSPI swung through two 3.5% to 3.7% daily falls and ended the week 2.1% lower, and Washington's public signals shifted from "no strikes before the midterms" to a US envoy saying a strike could come this weekend.

---

## 1. The Week's Arc

The week began with Brent at $109.55 on October 19 and Iran's seven point plan (Hormuz reopens on day six, talks on day seven) unanswered by Washington. Oil drifted near $109 until Wednesday. The break came on Thursday and Friday, when the Houthis claimed four strikes in 48 hours on the Riyadh defense ministry, Dammam's King Fahd airport, an Aramco site at Yanbu and Abha airport, two people died at Dammam, and Reuters reported that Aramco ordered its Dhahran headquarters evacuated. Last week's structural story was a widening front; this week's is that the front reached the core of Saudi administration and aviation.

Washington's posture moved without a decision. The week opened with Trump's pledge of no strikes before November 3 intact and the FT reporting that he blocked an Israeli strike campaign. By Friday Trump said the US is not negotiating and that "something will happen soon," the NYT reported plans for a short intensive campaign after the midterms, and on Saturday ambassador Tom Barrack said a strike could come as soon as this weekend while the WSJ reported the USS George Washington rearming with bunker busters. Iran answered by barring US warships from the Gulf, claiming mine hits on loaded supertankers, and leaking plans to cut Gulf undersea cables. The US ordered non-emergency staff to leave Saudi Arabia during the week, and Etihad cancelled Riyadh and Dammam flights through November 5. Diplomacy is thin: Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, Egypt and Turkey are meeting European ministers in Istanbul, and Pezeshkian meets Putin on October 27.

In Korea, KOSPI fell 3.73% on October 20, rebounded 3.3% on October 21, fell 3.52% on October 22 after the Bank of Korea held at 3.00% while signaling a hike, and recovered 0.3% on October 23 after a 4.12% intraday plunge. The government's response remains monitoring; the Saudi level 3 departure recommendation from October 11 stands.

## 5. Revisions and Lessons

No material correction to `instructions/korea-exposure.md` was needed. The October 24 brief's intraday Brent and WTI figures were corrected in the October 25 daily (settles $116.72 and $103.27), and its "highest since May 4" became April 29. Lessons: a Saturday run should always fetch the Friday settle first, since the October 24 brief's Friday-morning window missed it again despite last week's lesson; a Dhahran evacuation that two regional outlets reported without attribution was a wire story one day later, so unattributed Gulf reports of Aramco decisions deserve Medium confidence rather than Low; and the ledger cleanup, which should not need repeating, requires shorter deadlines on diplomatic indicators.
