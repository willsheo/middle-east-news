# Middle East Weekly Review

**November 16 to November 22, 2026**

- **Week in brief:** The blockade standoff turned into a daily tanker war off Khasab and a first exchange of fire between US and Iranian forces in months, then a pause when Trump said he would wait until after the New Year, and by the weekend Iran was pressing again with a damaging attack on Bahrain and a threat to break the blockade; the Saudi and Houthi war reached the ports, Iraqi forces entered Jurf al-Sakhar, and Brent fell 3.4% to $92.57 while the KOSPI lost 7.9% to 4,869.96 on a technology selloff.

---

## 1. The Week's Arc

Last week ended with both sides treating the blockade as a stable end state. This week showed that it is not stable at sea. UKMTO confirmed a ship struck near Khasab on almost every day from November 16: the tanker ON PHOENIX, the LPG tanker TARA GAS (hit twice, one seafarer missing), an ADNOC L&S vessel with 11 injured, then unnamed tankers on November 18, 20 and 21. Last week's pattern was Iranian mine claims that no one could confirm; this week's is real hits confirmed by a maritime body, which Iran still describes as mines. UKMTO put traffic through the strait about 65% below pre-war levels in the week to November 18.

On land the week had three phases. First came escalation: after a third IRGC drone attack on Bahrain in five days, US jets struck an IRGC missile site on Qeshm on November 17, and Iran's army answered against US air defences in Kuwait. Then came a pause: Trump told Israeli television he was "not rushing" and would see "after the new year starts," the Senate rejected a war powers resolution 48 to 51, and for two days neither side fired on the other. The pause ended on November 21 when an Iranian attack set a civilian industrial facility in Bahrain on fire, and Tehran said it would break the blockade by any action necessary, with tankers reported gathering at Kharg Island. Diplomacy stayed indirect: Araghchi in Muscat, US envoys with the Qataris in Berlin, a Qatari delegation in Tehran, and no meeting between US and Iranian officials.

What changed structurally is that the war's secondary fronts moved faster than its main one. The Saudi led coalition bombed Hodeidah port after a Houthi missile damaged the Public Investment Fund tower in Riyadh, and the Houthis named Jeddah and the other Saudi Red Sea ports as targets, which puts the kingdom's bypass route under explicit threat for the first time. In Iraq, federal forces entered Kataib Hezbollah's stronghold at Jurf al-Sakhar under a compromise, the first state presence there in about 12 years. In Israel, Eisenkot's bloc elected the Knesset speaker 66 to 52, and he said he will put an emergency government to a vote if unity talks fail, while Netanyahu, as caretaker, presses Washington for a new campaign against Iran.

In Korea the week was driven by technology shares, not the Gulf. The KOSPI fell below 5,000 on Tuesday, recovered, then dropped 5.21% on Friday to its lowest close in about ten months, with foreigners net selling in every session. The won barely moved. The Cheonghae Unit motion stayed before the National Assembly.

## 2. Indicator Scoreboard

21 indicators were opened this week (IND-20261116-1 through IND-20261122-3) and 19 were resolved during it: **12 confirmed** and **7 falsified**, none expired. Confirmed calls were concentrated in two areas: the tanker campaign, where the successive "another distinct vessel" indicators (IND-20261118-2, IND-20261119-2, IND-20261120-2, IND-20261121-2) each resolved within a day or two, and events with a set date or a clear physical test, such as the Knesset speaker vote (IND-20261111-3), the US strike (IND-20261118-1) and the Iraqi entry into Jurf al-Sakhar (IND-20261117-2). Falsified calls were the threshold and deadline ones: a US and Iranian meeting in Qatar (IND-20261111-1), Brent above $100 (IND-20261113-1), the won above 1,350 (IND-20261113-3), a named mine strike (IND-20261112-2), a named LNG carrier (IND-20261114-2), the KOSPI above 5,383.80 (IND-20261111-2) and Trump's "war ends after the election" (IND-20260910-3). **19 indicators remain open**; the nearest deadlines are IND-20261119-1 (a second US strike) and IND-20261119-3 (won below 1,325) on November 26, then five on November 27 including Brent below $90 (IND-20261115-1) and the KOSPI below 4,800 (IND-20261121-3). IND-20260714-4 (Qatari LNG loadings) is still open with no deadline and no fresh figure.

**Calibration note:** The hit rate rose from 3 of 19 last week to 12 of 19. That is partly real: last week's lesson, to stop waiting for diplomatic announcements and to track observable costs, was applied. It is also partly easy scoring. Four of the twelve confirmations were the same daily question about the next tanker, asked in a week when ships were being hit daily. Indicators that resolve in a day tell the reader little; next week's maritime indicators should ask something harder, such as a named Korean linked vessel, a casualty, or a change in weekly traffic. Extreme market thresholds ($100 Brent, 1,350 won) keep failing; the market has traded in a narrow band for three weeks, and thresholds should sit closer to it.

## 4. Slow Threads

- **Saudi Red Sea ports (IND-20261121-1):** the Houthi threat to Jeddah has not been carried out, but it now hangs over Yanbu, where Saudi crude for Korea loads. Insurer and shipping line responses are the thing to watch.
- **The blockade itself (IND-20261122-1):** CENTCOM widened its rules to ship to ship transfers; Iran is starting rolling power cuts and talks of a convoy. The blockade is moving from background condition to the likely point of the next clash.
- **Kataib Hezbollah (IND-20261120-1):** the state is inside Jurf al-Sakhar, the group keeps its weapons, and no unit is confirmed to have left.
- **Iran's nuclear file (IND-20261113-2, IND-20261114-3, IND-20261115-2):** the IAEA Board met with inspectors still barred from all sites but Bushehr; the US and European powers demanded access and no new resolution is reported.
- **Israeli government formation:** Eisenkot's mandate runs to early December; talks with Likud begin Sunday and a minority government vote is the fallback.
- **Aden (IND-20261117-1):** Houthi forces are reported close to the city but not within 50 km.
- **Cheonghae Unit expansion (IND-20261112-1):** still awaiting an Assembly vote, due by the end of the month.
- **Qatari LNG loadings (IND-20260714-4):** no fresh weekly figure for another week.

## 5. Revisions and Lessons

No material correction to `instructions/korea-exposure.md` was needed. One claim was walked back: the November 21 brief relayed a Reuters line that Hormuz crude flows were near pre-war levels, which UKMTO's weekly traffic figure does not support; unquantified flow statements will be labelled Medium at most and not used in the implications section until a count backs them. Lessons: Korean market moves this week had causes outside the Gulf, and the briefs were right to say so each day instead of fitting them to the war; the standing assumption that "Iranian incident claims are unverifiable" needs splitting, because mine claims remain unconfirmed while projectile strikes are now confirmed almost daily; and a pause announced by one leader is not a ceasefire, so quiet days should be recorded as observations, not as a trend, until they pass a week.
