# Middle East Weekly Review

**October 26 to November 1, 2026**

- **Week in brief:** Israel voted without producing a majority for either bloc (53, 48 and 19 seats), Iran moved from "no talks" to calling Vance's letter a positive step while still planning no meeting, Brent ended October at $117.64 (up 13.6% on the month, up about 0.8% on the week), and the KOSPI swung from a 6,012.44 record-day close to 5,730.74, ending the week 3.3% higher.

---

## 1. The Week's Arc

The week opened with Egypt's parliament authorizing military support for Saudi Arabia, a Houthi missile fired at Riyadh's airport hours after it reopened, and Saudi, Pakistani and Turkish officials meeting Iranians in Tehran. Israel voted on October 27 and the count settled at 53 for Netanyahu's bloc, 48 for Eisenkot's and 19 for Arab parties, with Netanyahu alleging a stolen vote and the Knesset due to be sworn in on November 10. Last week's structural story was a Houthi front reaching Saudi Arabia's core; this week's is that the front stayed active while diplomacy acquired a channel: Qatari mediators in Tehran, a Vance letter that the WSJ reported on October 28, and an Iranian aide in Doha by Saturday.

Maritime risk shifted from Iranian claims to deniable attacks. On October 29 the tanker Louise 1 burned after limpet mines were attached off Khasab, unclaimed, and on Friday the IRGC said four more tankers hit mines on "unauthorised routes," which CENTCOM and Treasury Secretary Bessent contradicted by saying routes are cleared and transit is near prewar. Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority turned on Korea's Sinokor, announcing legal action against all its ships from November 1. On Washington's side Trump kept saying Iran "wants to talk" and the war ends "very soon," the Pentagon briefed post midterm options, and on Saturday Rubio said no decision has been made. The week ends one election day short of the pledge window: the November 3 midterms, then the strike tests of November 4 and 6.

In Korea, the KOSPI fell 1.46% to 5,469.47 on October 26, rose 3.09% on October 27, jumped 6.63% to 6,012.44 on October 28 after SK Hynix's record profit, then fell 1.24% and 3.48% as Samsung's deferred buyback details and Alphabet's AI spending worries hit chips. Seoul's visible response to the Sinokor warning was silence.

## 5. Revisions and Lessons

No material correction to `instructions/korea-exposure.md` was needed. The October 31 daily's Brent ($117.62 intraday, no settle) and WTI (unreported) were corrected in the November 1 daily with Reuters settles of $117.64 and $104.12. Lessons: a Friday settle missed on a weekend run can be recovered the next morning from Reuters via CNBC, so the first search on any Saturday or Sunday run should be the prior day's settle; the expiring contract (December) and the active contract (January) differed by $4.21, so the series should record which contract it follows; and wire claims by the IRGC about tanker hits should stay at Low confidence until an operator, UKMTO or JMIC confirms.
