# Middle East Weekly Review

**September 28 to October 4, 2026**

- **Week in brief:** Washington's rejection of Iran's seven day Hormuz plan carried into a week of stalled mediation through Qatar, with no Iranian reply to the US counterproposal by Sunday, while the Saudi and Houthi war moved from missile exchanges toward a planned Yemeni ground offensive and air raids on Sanaa, the G7 answered the fuel squeeze with a 100 million barrel stock release that barely moved Brent, and Washington kept adding forces ahead of the November midterms.

---

## 1. The Week's Arc

The week opened with Iran hardening rather than moving: Araghchi said Tehran was awaiting mediators' "definitive views" on Trump's rejection, the army chief said the strait stays closed until US economic warfare ends, and the IRGC's claimed capture of a second US drone was dismissed by CENTCOM (Sept 28). Brent spiked to $108.83 intraday before settling at $105.28 on Sept 28, the first session after Chuseok. Mediation then moved to Qatar: Araghchi met Qatari mediators in New York, carried a US proposal back to Iran's cabinet, and Trump denied an Axios report that he would ease sanctions (Sept 30 to Oct 1). Iran's reply stayed unannounced through the end of the week, and Washington and Tehran remained split on sequencing, with Hormuz first versus nuclear first.

The Saudi and Houthi front gained weight. Houthi missiles and drones kept reaching Saudi cities, including Khamis Mushait and Jazan, and a Saudi strike on a Taiz market killed at least seven (Sept 28). On Oct 2 reports emerged that Riyadh is planning a Yemeni led ground offensive on the Bab el-Mandeb coast with Saudi air support, and on Oct 3 Saudi jets struck Sanaa while the Houthis claimed a hit on an Aramco site south of Riyadh. US involvement stayed indirect: intelligence help, a third carrier group reported, and a Theodore Roosevelt carrier group and Makin Island amphibious group heading out with about 9,000 personnel, with Trump telling a rally the war should end "right after the election."

Energy policy answered the price level rather than the war. The US offered up to 40 million barrels in SPR exchanges (Sept 30), and on Oct 2 the G7 agreed to release up to 100 million barrels of crude and diesel over four months with a frontloaded diesel tranche. Brent barely reacted, settling at $102.25. The US military completed its Iraq withdrawal and the coalition mission there ended on Sept 30, while the flydubai attack, with reports pointing to the co-pilot's radicalization rather than Iran, and several unattributed tanker hits near Hormuz kept the shipping picture dangerous.
