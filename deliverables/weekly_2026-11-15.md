# Middle East Weekly Review

**November 9 to November 15, 2026**

- **Week in brief:** Talk of a Qatari deal gave way to a blockade standoff, as Trump said the war was effectively over and then that the US did not need a deal, Iran claimed tanker mine strikes every day from November 10 to 14 and its first attack on a US facility in Bahrain in months, and an IAEA report said diversion of Iran's nuclear material can no longer be ruled out; Brent fell 2.5% on the week to $95.84 while the KOSPI recovered 4.6% to 5,290.53.

---

## 1. The Week's Arc

The week opened with diplomacy at its high point: Trump hosted Qatar's prime minister on November 9 and called the war "effectively over" the next day, and Brent dipped below $90 for the first time since August. By midweek the frame had flipped. Iran said the strait stays shut until its seven conditions are met, Rezaei ruled out shipping enriched uranium abroad and denied any planned Vance meeting, and on November 12 Trump wrote that the US no longer needs a deal because it holds Iran's money and the strait. What changed structurally versus last week is that both sides now describe the blockade as a stable end state rather than a bridge to a text: Washington is content to wait, and Tehran is raising the cost of waiting through the sea lanes rather than at the table. The November 15 deadlines for a Qatari announcement passed with nothing, and Reuters reports Trump has told advisers he may hold off on new strikes unless provoked.

At sea, Iran claimed mine strikes on tankers using "unauthorized" routes on each day from November 10 to 14, more than ten ships in all, none named and none independently confirmed; the one verified hit was a projectile strike off Ras al Khaimah on November 11. On November 13 the IRGC claimed it destroyed a US radar in Bahrain after a US strike on a telecom tower in Sirik, and CENTCOM said both drones were shot down. The Houthi campaign against Saudi Arabia widened to claims on the East West pipeline, Yanbu and Aramco in Riyadh, while Riyadh confirmed only interceptions and the UN Security Council extended Houthi sanctions. In Israel, the 26th Knesset was sworn in with Eisenkot holding the mandate; Netanyahu, now a caretaker, sent the Mossad chief to Washington to lobby for renewed strikes and tried to lure Eisenkot into a unity government with the Iran threat.

In Korea, markets stabilized after the previous week's rout: foreigners turned net buyers on November 10 and 11 for the first time in eight sessions, the KOSPI rose 4.6% on the week despite Friday's Nvidia driven selloff, and the won firmed. On policy, the cabinet approved extending the Cheonghae Unit's operating area to waters south of Oman with patrol aircraft and mine clearance equipment, which Iran publicly warned against; the motion awaits National Assembly consent.

**Calibration note:** For the third week running, indicators that waited for a diplomatic announcement falsified; the base rate now says "no announcement" is the default and any such call should be framed that way. Calls premised on Iranian claims being confirmed (mine strikes, Saudi damage) also keep failing, which is information in itself: Iran and the Houthis are generating unverifiable incidents at a high rate. The market calls did better this week, as the KOSPI recovery and Brent's rebound above $95 were both called correctly.

## 5. Revisions and Lessons

No material correction to `instructions/korea-exposure.md` was needed. Lessons: weekday market data from the previous session should be recorded on the following day's run, as this week's Monday settles had to be backfilled; the absence of a named vessel across more than ten Iranian mine claims should now be treated as a pattern rather than a gap in reporting, and Section 1 confidence labels will keep "Low" on the event until a maritime body confirms one; and with Washington and Tehran both treating the blockade as an end state, deadline based diplomatic indicators should be replaced with indicators on the blockade's costs, such as transit counts, war risk premiums and Korea's import bill.
